# A Piece of Whole — Content & Curriculum

**Format:** All curriculum content is seeded into the database via admin CMS. It can be edited after launch. This document provides the complete starter content for the default 8-week cohort.

---

## 8-Week Starter Cohort Curriculum

**Cohort name (starter):** "The Beginning"
**Intended duration:** 8 weeks
**Pacing:** One theme per week. Practices are self-paced within the week.

---

### Week 1 — Arriving

**Theme:** You are here. Before anything else, you arrive.

**Circle Prompt:**
> "What brought you here? Not the polished version — the real one. What made you say yes to this?"

**Facilitator Note:**
> This week holds the entry. Let the shares be whatever they are. Resist the urge to synthesize or redirect early. Let people speak into the space and feel that the space holds them.

**Practice Title:** The Body as Home

**Practice Type:** Audio + Text

**Practice Text:**
> Find a seat. Not a perfect one — just one where you can feel the ground holding you.
>
> Notice your feet. Are they on the floor? What does that contact feel like?
>
> Place one hand on your chest. Not to fix anything. Just to know that something is there — breath, heat, a heartbeat.
>
> You don't have to feel anything in particular right now. You just have to be here.
>
> That's it. That's the whole practice for this week. Coming back, again and again, to the fact that you have a body and it is in a place.
>
> Spend five minutes. Or fifteen. Or just a few breaths. The duration is less important than the return.

**Audio guidance:** 8–10 minute guided version of the above (facilitator or narrator reads, with silence woven in).

**Journal Prompt (Week 1):**
> What does "arriving" mean for you right now? Where are you coming from?

---

### Week 2 — The Nervous System Isn't a Problem to Solve

**Theme:** Understanding the body's responses as wisdom, not weakness.

**Circle Prompt:**
> "When does your body tell you that something is wrong before your mind catches up? What does that feel like for you?"

**Facilitator Note:**
> Many participants may feel shame around their nervous system responses. Help normalize activation without collapsing into it. This week isn't about fixing — it's about witnessing.

**Practice Title:** Meeting Activation

**Practice Type:** Text + Audio

**Practice Text:**
> Think of a moment recently when you felt activated — stressed, anxious, braced for something.
>
> Without going back into the moment itself, ask: where did you feel it? Chest, shoulders, jaw, gut?
>
> Now, instead of trying to calm it down, just get curious. What was that activation trying to do for you? What was it protecting?
>
> You don't have to have an answer. The question is the practice.
>
> When you're ready, take three slow breaths. Not to make the activation go away — just to let your system know the moment has passed.

**Journal Prompt (Week 2):**
> When did you learn that your body's responses were a problem? Who taught you that?

---

### Week 3 — Co-Regulation Is Not Weakness

**Theme:** We are wired for each other. Needing others is not a failure.

**Circle Prompt:**
> "Tell us about a moment when someone's presence — physically or otherwise — helped you regulate. What made it possible?"

**Facilitator Note:**
> Co-regulation is the heart of the app. This week, normalize interdependence. Many participants will have internalized hyper-independence as strength. Hold space for the grief of that.

**Practice Title:** Being Witnessed

**Practice Type:** Text

**Practice Text:**
> Think of someone — real or remembered — whose presence makes you feel safer.
>
> It doesn't have to be a dramatic relationship. It could be someone who simply noticed you. A teacher who used your name well. A stranger who held a door and looked you in the eyes.
>
> Let yourself feel, briefly, what it is to be seen by someone who wishes you well.
>
> This is co-regulation. It is not magic. It is not weakness. It is the oldest technology humans have.
>
> Now consider: what keeps you from this? What makes it hard to let people in?
>
> You don't have to share this with anyone. Just sit with it.

**Journal Prompt (Week 3):**
> What do you lose when you let someone in? What do you gain?

---

### Week 4 — The Inner Critic Isn't the Enemy

**Theme:** Parts-based awareness. Every voice inside has a job.

**Circle Prompt:**
> "What does your inner critic say most often? And what do you think it's actually trying to protect you from?"

**Facilitator Note:**
> This week draws lightly on IFS-adjacent language. Not everyone will have familiarity with it. Use plain language. The point is curiosity, not technique. Gently discourage participants from trying to "fix" or "silence" parts.

**Practice Title:** Getting Curious About the Critic

**Practice Type:** Audio + Text

**Practice Text:**
> Close your eyes if that's comfortable. Take a breath.
>
> Let the most familiar critical thought come forward. Not to argue with it — just to notice it.
>
> Now, instead of listening to what it's saying, look at it. How old does it feel? How loud? Does it feel tight or sharp or exhausted?
>
> Ask it, gently: "What are you afraid would happen if you stopped?"
>
> You may get nothing. You may get a feeling. You may get a word.
>
> Whatever comes, let it be there. Thank this part for trying.
>
> Take another breath. Come back.

**Journal Prompt (Week 4):**
> If your inner critic had a face or a shape, what would it look like? What does it need from you?

---

### Week 5 — Grief Is a Form of Love

**Theme:** Acknowledging loss as part of moving forward.

**Circle Prompt:**
> "What are you grieving right now — even something small, something you haven't had permission to grieve before?"

**Facilitator Note:**
> This is often the heaviest week. Hold the space extra gently. If shares are intense, slow the pace. Remind the circle that witnessing is enough — they don't need to fix each other's grief. If anyone seems to need more support than the circle can hold, reach out privately.

**Practice Title:** Giving Grief Its Due

**Practice Type:** Text + Audio

**Practice Text:**
> Grief doesn't always look like crying. Sometimes it looks like numbness. Or anger. Or exhaustion you can't explain.
>
> Think of something you've lost — a relationship, a version of yourself, a future you expected, a place, a time.
>
> Let yourself acknowledge it, just for a moment. Not to wallow — but to honor.
>
> Say, quietly or in your mind: "I lost this. It mattered."
>
> That's it. You don't have to do anything with it. You just have to let it be real.

**Journal Prompt (Week 5):**
> What grief have you been carrying that hasn't had a name? Name it today.

---

### Week 6 — Agency Is a Practice

**Theme:** From reaction to response. Reclaiming authorship of your life.

**Circle Prompt:**
> "Tell us about a moment recently where you chose something — even something small — instead of just reacting. What made that possible?"

**Facilitator Note:**
> Agency is not about control — it's about authorship. Be careful not to let this week become about willpower or productivity. The frame is: even in constrained circumstances, there are small choices. Those choices compound.

**Practice Title:** The Space Between Stimulus and Response

**Practice Type:** Text

**Practice Text:**
> Viktor Frankl wrote: "Between stimulus and response there is a space. In that space is our power to choose our response."
>
> This week, we practice finding that space.
>
> When something happens that would normally trigger a reaction — a message, a comment, a task, a feeling — pause. Just for three seconds.
>
> In those three seconds, ask: "What do I actually want to do here?"
>
> You might still do the reactive thing. That's fine. The practice is the pause — not the outcome.
>
> Try it once today. Once is enough.

**Journal Prompt (Week 6):**
> Where in your life do you feel most like you're just reacting? What would authorship look like there?

---

### Week 7 — You Are Embedded

**Theme:** You exist in a place, a time, and a community. That's not a burden — it's a source.

**Circle Prompt:**
> "What community — neighborhood, city, family, culture — are you embedded in right now? What do you love about it? What troubles you?"

**Facilitator Note:**
> This week bridges from the inner work to the civic. It should feel like a natural expansion, not a gear shift. The civic isn't external to the personal — it's the largest container of our personal lives.

**Practice Title:** Walking as Civic Practice

**Practice Type:** Text

**Practice Text:**
> Go for a walk. It doesn't have to be long.
>
> As you walk, notice: Who else is here? What has been built? What has been neglected? What is being cared for?
>
> Notice what you feel as you notice these things. Not to analyze — just to feel.
>
> You are embedded in this. The streets, the buildings, the people — they are not backdrop. They are part of your life.
>
> When you return, write down three things you noticed that you'd never paid attention to before.

**Journal Prompt (Week 7):**
> What do you feel called to contribute to the community you're embedded in? What holds you back?

---

### Week 8 — Carrying It Forward

**Theme:** The practice doesn't end. The circle doesn't disappear. What do you take with you?

**Circle Prompt:**
> "What is one thing from this cohort that you want to remember in six months? What is one thing you want to do differently in your life?"

**Facilitator Note:**
> This week is a threshold. Hold it as a ceremony, not a summary. Let the shares be forward-looking and backward-looking at once. Thank each person specifically if you can. Remind them that the circle, in some form, continues — in how they show up, in the people they hold with more care.

**Practice Title:** The Spiral

**Practice Type:** Text

**Practice Text:**
> Growth isn't linear. It spirals. You will return to grief. You will return to the critic. You will return to the moment where you forget that co-regulation is an option.
>
> That's not failure. That's the nature of practice.
>
> What you carry from here isn't a set of tools or a list of insights. It's a different relationship with the question. You've learned to ask: "What does my body know? What does this part of me need? What is the community asking of me?"
>
> Those questions don't have final answers. They just keep getting richer.
>
> You are a piece of whole. So is everyone in this circle.

**Journal Prompt (Week 8):**
> Write a letter to yourself, to be opened in six months. What do you want to remember?

## Static Civic Modules

Civic lessons are optional, nonpartisan, and values-based. They are available throughout the 8 weeks, not locked to specific weeks.

### Lesson 1 — How Local Government Actually Works

**Category:** Local government

**Content summary:**
- The difference between city/county/school board/special district
- Who makes decisions about your neighborhood
- How to find your representatives (whitehouse.gov/contact, usa.gov/local-governments as reference structure)
- What a city council meeting looks like and how to attend
- The planning and zoning process — where most local decisions actually happen

**Civic prompt (non-required reflection):**
> "Who are your local elected officials? Do you know their names? How long have they held office?"

---

### Lesson 2 — Participation Beyond Voting

**Category:** Participation

**Content summary:**
- Why voting matters and why it's not enough
- Public comment periods: what they are and how to use them
- Neighborhood associations, HOAs, block clubs — their power and limits
- Showing up vs. writing vs. organizing — spectrum of participation
- Time, capacity, and triage: how to participate without burning out

**Civic prompt:**
> "What is one form of civic participation you've done? What is one you've never tried?"

---

### Lesson 3 — Community as Infrastructure

**Category:** Community

**Content summary:**
- Social capital: bridging vs. bonding
- The research on community cohesion and individual wellbeing
- Third places: what they are, why they're declining, and why it matters
- Informal community care (mutual aid, neighboring, skill sharing)
- How personal regulation connects to community resilience

**Civic prompt:**
> "What third place — a library, coffee shop, park, church, community center — has mattered to you? What made it a place?"

---

### Lesson 4 — When Systems Fail

**Category:** Community

**Content summary:**
- What systemic failure looks like (housing, healthcare access, food access — local examples)
- The difference between charity and structural change
- How to hold complexity: critique a system without losing compassion for the people in it
- What individuals can realistically do, and what requires collective action
- Finding your lane: where your skills, your time, and the need overlap

**Civic prompt:**
> "What local problem troubles you most? What would it take to address it — at the individual level, and at the structural level?"

## Circle Guides (Facilitator Resources)

These guides help facilitators hold the circle space. They are in the admin CMS and visible to facilitators but not participants.

### Opening a Circle Session

> Begin with a brief moment of arrival. Invite participants to take a breath before reading. Acknowledge the week's theme without summarizing what people should feel. Read the circle prompt aloud (if facilitating synchronously) or post it cleanly (if async). Then step back and let the circle speak.

### Holding Space for Difficulty

> If a share is heavy, resist the impulse to fix, reframe, or redirect. Acknowledge it directly: "That took courage to share." Then hold the silence before the next share appears. Remind the circle that support, not advice, is the practice.

### When a Share Crosses a Line

> If a share describes active crisis, self-harm, or harm to others: remove it quietly, reach out to the person privately, connect them to crisis resources, and escalate to admin. Never publicly address a safety situation in the circle.

### Closing a Cohort

> Thank each participant by name or by something specific they contributed. Remind the circle that the skills practiced here — arriving, witnessing, co-regulating — belong to them now. They take the circle with them.
